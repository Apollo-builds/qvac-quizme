The Water Cycle

The water cycle describes how water moves through Earth's atmosphere,
land, and oceans. Energy from the sun drives the cycle.

Evaporation: liquid water heats up and becomes water vapor, a gas.
This happens mostly from oceans, lakes, and rivers.

Condensation: water vapor cools and turns back into tiny liquid
droplets, forming clouds.

Precipitation: when droplets in clouds grow heavy enough, they fall as
rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

Collection: fallen water gathers in oceans, lakes, rivers, and
groundwater, and the cycle begins again.

Transpiration is another source of water vapor: plants release water
through their leaves.

The water cycle is essential for weather, climate, and all life on
Earth because it continually recycles the same finite supply of water.
